## Historical Animal Observation Records by Bavarian Forestry Offices (1845)

Rehbein, Malte, Escobari Vargas, Andrea Belen, Fischer, Sarah, Güntsch, Anton, Haas, Bettina, Matheisen, Giada, Perschl, Tobias, Wieshuber, Alois, Engel, Thore

Published 2024-10-07

Licensed cc-by-4.0



In 1845, under the scientific direction of Andreas Wagner, the Bavarian government recorded the occurrence of 44 selected vertebrate species across the entire country. To this end, Wagner had a survey questionnaire sent to all 119 forestry offices in the state. The foresters' responses were now systematically recorded and analyzed for the first time. This data set represents the result of this survey. Among other things, it contains 5,464 geo-coded animal observation data.
The data is the result of an interdisciplinary collaboration between scientists from the Chair of Computational Humanities at the University of Passau, the Directorate General of the Bavarian State Archives Munich, the German Centre for Integrative Biodiversity Research (iDiv) Halle-Jena-Leipzig, the Center for Biodiversity Informatics and Collection Data Integration at the Botanical Garden Berlin, and the NFDI4Biodiversity consortium.

Published 2024-05-13

Licensed cc-by-4.0



In 1845, a survey was conducted across all Bavarian forestry districts. The foresters in charge were required to complete a tabular form documenting the animal species present within their specific forest district. The project's objective is to render this historical observational data accessible for biodiversity research purposes.

## NFDI4Biodiversity - Status Report Use Case Projects

Gemeinholzer, Birgit, Frenzel, Mark, Bonn, Aletta, Fischer, Sarah, Holetschek, Jörg, Engel, Thore, Friedrichs-Manthey, Martin, Schomburg, Christoph, Ebert, Barbara

Published 2023-09-07

Licensed cc-by-4.0



Strengthening Germany&#39;s Biodiversity Data Network: User Engagement in NFDI4Biodiversity

The availability of biodiversity data&nbsp;is vital for informed decisions on sustainable use and conservation of biodiversity. NFDI4Biodiversity focuses on enabling biodiversity data exchange among stakeholders.

In collaboration with 50 partner institutions, NFDI4Biodiversity addresses diverse biodiversity facets, driven by partner needs. This report covers a 2.5-year user engagement phase, emphasizing 26 use cases adhering to FAIR principles for data mobilization and integration. The report highlights challenges in networking due to varying stakeholder needs and the need for integration in the biodiversity data landscape.

This report outlines NFDI4Biodiversity&#39;s efforts to strengthen Germany&#39;s biodiversity data network through user engagement, underscoring the importance of collaboration in biodiversity understanding and conservation.

## R scripts for harmonizing Odonata occurrence data from German federal states

Engel, Thore, Bosch, Maya

Published 2026-08-26

Licensed mit-license



This repository contains R scripts that harmonize and merge Odonata (dragonfly and damselfly) occurrence data from the German federal states. The records were compiled for the DRAGON project, which analyses distribution trends of European Odonata. Because the German source data are particularly heterogeneous — regional databases differing in schema, spatial resolution, date conventions and taxonomic naming — they were cleaned and harmonized separately before being integrated into the DRAGON database alongside other European countries. A subset was also made available for the German national Red List of Odonata.
The occurrence data themselves are not included in this archive. Data sharing beyond the DRAGON project was not permitted by the contributing data providers, and the compiled dataset is held under restricted access. What is published here is the harmonization code, released so that comparable data mobilization efforts can build on this work rather than start from scratch.
The scripts/data_cleaning folder holds one script per federal state, each mapping that dataset onto a common target schema and documenting its particularities in inline comments; data for Berlin were obtained from GBIF rather than a regional database. merging.R combines the cleaned datasets into a single harmonized table and maps their spatial coverage, and Rote Liste export.R produces the subset used for the national Red List.
The data were compiled from regional databases with the help of the Gesellschaft deutschsprachiger Odonatologen e.V. (GdO) and the German National Research Data Infrastructure for Biodiversity (NFDI4Biodiversity); the full list of contributing providers is given in the README. This work was supported by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG) under grant agreement 442032008 (NFDI4Biodiversity), part of the National Research Data Infrastructure (NFDI) programme in Germany.
