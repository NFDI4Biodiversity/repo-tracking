## Taxonomic name resolution

Schellenberger Costa, David

Published 2024-06-08

Licensed cc-by-4.0



In this hands-on workshop, I present basic information on taxonomic name resolution. The hands-on part helps users to implement name parsing, vernacular name resolution and taxonomic name resolution for their own data. We mainly use the GBIF API, but also the Leipzig Catalogue of Vascular Plants (LCVP) as an example for using an offline resource for name resolution. Coding is done in R, using the data.table, rgbif, and RJSONIO packages.

## Template for the NFDI4Biodiversity & iDiv Seasonal School on Data Management in Ecology and Environmental Science 2024

Röder, Juliane, Signer, Johannes, Scherreiks, Pascal, Figueiredo, Ludmilla, Kusch, Erik, Linares Gómez, Jimena, Müller, Wolfgang, Nozik, Alexandra, Schellenberger Costa, David, Scherer, Cédric, Schindler, Uwe, Tschink, Daniel

Published 2025-06-03

Licensed cc-by-4.0



This document describes a one-week course program for a Seasonal School on research data management (RDM) from 2024 which was organised for the second time by partners from the NFDI4Biodiversity consortium. The course aimed to provide young scientists in ecology and environmental science with state-of-the-art skills and knowledge in handling scientific data throughout the data life cycle. Lectures and practical sessions were given by experts from renowned universities, data centers, and research institutions in the environmental research community, covering general aspects of RDM and aspects specifically important for handling ecological data. The participants were introduced to Jupyter notebooks and learned how to combine software tools like Jupyter, R, GitHub, spreadsheet tools, and NFDI4Biodiversity Tools and Services. The course was conducted remotely and targeted PhD students (R1). This publication summarises detailed information about the structure, organisation, and lessons learned, and is supposed to be used as a guide for similar events.
We list all course materials (presentation slides, video recordings, code, Jupyter notebooks, trainings data sets) and accounts required to follow the course program in your own pace. Additional code and training data sets are available here:&nbsp;https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15594632. The course requires a minimum of programming skills in R (Python is optional).
